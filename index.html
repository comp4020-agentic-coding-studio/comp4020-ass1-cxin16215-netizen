How rebirth works

After you watched one full cycle, here is what makes Turritopsis dohrnii unusual among jellyfish.

  1. Stress, not a magic timer. Starvation, injury, or aging can push a medusa into decline. In most jellyfish species, that decline ends the animal’s life.
  2. The body settles and remakes itself. Instead of dying, the medusa can sink, retract its tentacles, and form a cyst-like stage on a surface. Over roughly a day or two, that mass reorganises into a polyp — the earlier, anchored body plan.
  3. Cells change job (transdifferentiation). Medusa tissues and polyp tissues are not the same. Specialised cells are reprogrammed into different specialised cells, so the animal can rebuild a whole new body plan without going back through an egg.
  4. The colony can release medusae again. Once the polyp is established, it can bud new jellyfish. Genetically it is still the same lineage — a fresh medusa generation grown from the returned polyp stage.

Biologists call this reverse development. It does not mean the animal never dies — disease, predation, and accident still kill it — but it can escape death by old age in a way almost no other animal can.

The loop closes — once
