# Assignment 1 reflection

I wanted this to feel like a nature documentary — real immersion, not a
diagram with a slider under it. That ambition is also what made the first
version wrong: four stages in, every check passed and it still read as a
small demo.

The breakthrough was realising I had two separate problems and had been
treating them as one.

The first was a principle I held on purpose. This is an explainer, so the
atmosphere must never compete with the thing being explained. That is why
the loop back to polyp is never described in text — it has to be discovered,
not read — and why every ambient layer is non-interactive.

The second was a real ceiling. Hand-authored SVG driven by script cannot
match what proper motion-design tooling produces, and there is almost no
usable footage of *Turritopsis dohrnii* at any stage. Once I stopped
treating that as a failure to push through, I could design around it:
discrete button-steps give each transition room to be a designed event,
where a continuous drag has no transition at all; and where I could not
source real photographs, I named the stand-in species honestly instead of
implying they were the same animal.

What I understand now is how wide the gap still is between what I want to
make and what I am actually able to make. At least I can name it: what is
missing is motion tooling and real source material, not the idea. I will do
the best I can with what I can reach today, and try to push past it from
there.
